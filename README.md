## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
We propose FarmLink, a digital marketplace connecting farmers and Farmer Producer Organizations (FPOs) directly with consumers, retailers, hotels, and other bulk buyers. Farmers can list available produce, quantities, grades, and prices. Buyers can compare listings and place orders through the platform.
FarmLink will coordinate pickup and delivery through local transport partners and collection centers. Demand forecasting will help farmers and FPOs plan what to supply, while route optimization will help logistics partners plan efficient deliveries.
This approach reduces reliance on multiple intermediaries, gives farmers access to more buyers, and helps consumers and businesses find produce at competitive prices. The platform will also show clear pricing and order details so participants can make informed decisions.

## Technical Approach
The platform will include:
- Mobile application and web portal: For farmers, FPOs, buyers, and logistics partners.
- Marketplace services: To manage listings, orders, payments, and buyer–seller communication.
- Demand forecasting: Use past orders, seasonal patterns, and available market data to estimate demand.
- Route optimization: Group nearby deliveries and suggest efficient routes based on order locations and vehicle capacity.
- Logistics coordination: Match orders with collection points and available transport partners.
- Prototype workflow: Farmer lists produce → buyer places an order → platform coordinates pickup → logistics partner delivers → both parties confirm completion.
The initial prototype can focus on one crop category and a limited service area, then expand based on feedback and operating results.

## Feasibility and Viability
The solution can be developed using commonly available smartphones, web services, mapping tools, and cloud infrastructure. It can begin as a small pilot with a few FPOs, local buyers, and transport partners before expanding to additional regions.
Potential challenges include inconsistent internet access, differences in produce quality, delivery delays, and limited trust between new buyers and sellers. These can be addressed through assisted onboarding, clear quality and quantity details, order tracking, delivery confirmation, and a dispute-handling process.
The marketplace can remain viable through a modest transaction fee or logistics service charge, with transparent pricing shown before an order is confirmed. Pilot results can guide decisions about pricing and expansion.

## Impact and Benefits
- Farmers and FPOs: Access to more buyers, clearer price information, and a chance to retain a larger share of the sale price.
- Consumers and bulk buyers: More choice, competitive prices, and better visibility into produce availability and delivery.
- Supply chain: Fewer unnecessary handoffs and improved coordination between sellers, buyers, and transport partners.
- Environment: Better route planning and combined deliveries may reduce unnecessary travel and fuel use.
The pilot can measure farmer earnings per sale, buyer prices, delivery time, order completion rates, and the number of intermediaries involved.
